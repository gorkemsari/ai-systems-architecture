## About This Handbook

This handbook covers the architecture of integrating LLM-based capabilities into production systems so that they are secure, scalable, observable, and cost-effective. It is written for solution and software architects and senior engineers who already build distributed systems and now have to make them work with a component that is probabilistic by nature.

Model training, machine learning mathematics, and research-level topics are deliberately out of scope; fine-tuning appears only as an architectural and integration decision. Every page favors durable principles and patterns over specific products, and names tools as examples of a mechanism rather than as recommendations.

This handbook is the companion of the [Distributed Systems Handbook](https://gorkemsari.github.io/distributed-systems/en/). Primitives such as idempotency, sagas, backpressure, caching, and consistency models are explained there once; pages here apply them to AI systems and link to the canonical page instead of repeating it.

## Reading Paths

- **Architects** start with Parts I and II, then read VI, VII, and XIV before designing anything for production.
- **Platform engineers** focus on Parts IX, X, and XI.
- **Security engineers** go to Part VII, then IV (retrieval authorization) and V (agent isolation).
- **Tech leads shipping a first feature** follow III, IV, VI, and VIII in that order.
